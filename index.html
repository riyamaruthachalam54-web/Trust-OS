The problem

Manual review doesn't scale, and black-box AI doesn't survive an audit.

Volume outpaces headcountThousands of cases arrive daily across marketplaces, fintech, and social platforms — far more than investigators can review consistently.
Evidence lives everywhereSignals are scattered across payment systems, chat logs, images, and policy documents that nobody has time to cross-reference by hand.
Policy drifts between reviewersThe same violation can get two different outcomes depending on who's on shift and how they read the rulebook that day.
Regulators want a paper trail"The model flagged it" isn't a defensible answer anymore — every high-impact decision needs a reason a human can stand behind.
Architecture

One case, seven checkpoints.

A case doesn't reach a decision until it's passed through every stage below, in order — that's the point.

01

Event intake

A flagged listing, transaction, or account enters the investigation portal with its full context attached.

Investigation Portal
02

Parallel agent analysis

The Evidence Agent correlates records across systems, the Policy Agent maps the case to the relevant clauses, and the Vision Agent inspects any submitted imagery — simultaneously.

Evidence · Policy · Vision
03

Autonomous Risk Council

Agent findings are weighed against each other to produce a single risk position, with disagreements surfaced rather than silently averaged.

Risk Council
04

Compliance Decision Engine

The risk position is checked against enterprise policy thresholds to determine whether it can be closed automatically or needs a person.

Decision Engine
05

Human review, when it matters

High-impact or ambiguous cases stop here. A reviewer sees the full reasoning chain, not just a score.

Human-in-the-loop
06

Executive report

A plain-language summary of the case, the evidence, and the reasoning is generated for stakeholders who weren't in the investigation.

Reporting
07

Sealed audit log

The full decision path is written to an immutable log — the same stream you saw ticking in the hero above.

Why it matters

Speed and defensibility, not one instead of the other.

→

Faster turnaround

Agents do the correlation work up front, so reviewers spend their time on judgment, not searching.

→

Consistent policy

The same clause gets applied the same way, whether it's case one or case ten thousand.

→

Explainable by default

Every recommendation carries its own reasoning — nothing is a black box when someone asks why.

→

Human authority preserved

Automation handles volume; people still own every high-impact call.

→

Audit-ready by construction

The log isn't bolted on afterward — it's the same trail the system used to decide.

→

Scales without new headcount

Case volume grows against agent capacity, not against hiring plans.
